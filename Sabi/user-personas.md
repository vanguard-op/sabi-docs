# Sabi: user personas

## Overview

The audience segments by payment obligation and by channel, not by income alone. A market trader manages recurring levies and household bills on a mid-range Android phone. A feature-phone user pays by USSD with no data. A registered small-company owner needs verifiable receipts for tax and audit. A diaspora sender pays family obligations from abroad and wants proof of settlement. The four share one frustration: a payment that succeeds can still leave them without usable proof.

## Persona 1: Ngozi

**Tagline:** A market trader who settles levies and bills between customers and cannot afford a payment that disappears.
**Demographic:** 34, Onitsha, Anambra State. Runs a fabric stall in a main market. Secondary education. Mid-range Android phone on a monthly data bundle.
**Role:** Market trader and micro-business owner
**Tech Comfort:** Medium
**Research basis:** [KPMG and Orange smartphone study](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/ng/pdf/2026/09/Nigeria%20Smartphone%20Study%20-%20Orange%20Group%20and%20KPMG.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf) on fintech app adoption (up to 88% of surveyed users), [Techdom Africa](https://techdomafrica.com/super-apps-must-serve-local-realities/) on cash-in and cash-out agent networks, and [Naija on Point](https://naijaonpoint.com.ng/failed-transaction-how-palmpay-frustrated-trader-for-two-years-over-n15200/) on a trader's two-year failed-refund case.
**In their words:** "I made several efforts to recover the money, but all my attempts yielded no results. I called PalmPay several times about the issue. They promised to refund me but failed to do so." (a trader, via Naija on Point)

### Goals
- Pay market association levies and local government fees without leaving the stall.
- Buy electricity tokens and airtime for the household.
- Keep proof of every payment for the association and for her own records.
- Avoid losing money to failed or reversed transactions.

### Frustrations
- A failed payment can take weeks or years to refund, if it returns at all.
- Customer service is hard to reach and gives no firm timeline.
- She does not always know which agency or code collects a given levy.
- Proof of payment arrives as an SMS she deletes, or it never arrives.

### Behaviors
- Uses OPay or PalmPay for transfers and bill pay, and WhatsApp for customer orders.
- Keeps cash for small payments and uses an agent or PoS for cash-in.
- Pays during slow periods at the stall, on mobile data.
- Trusts word of mouth from other traders about which channel works.

### Scenario
A council levy is due, and Ngozi does not know which account collects it. She has lost a payment to a reversal before, so she wants a receipt before she closes her stall. She opens Sabi, types the levy name in Pidgin, confirms the amount it resolves to the council, pays through the licensed rail, and saves the receipt the agent hands back. If the agency does not confirm the payment, Sabi keeps the transaction open and tells her what it is doing, instead of leaving her to chase a refund on her own.

## Persona 2: Musa

**Tagline:** An okada rider who banks and pays by USSD on a feature phone, with no data and no patience for long menus.
**Demographic:** 28, Kano. Rides a commercial motorcycle. Feature phone only. Comfortable in Hausa and Pidgin, limited English literacy.
**Role:** Commercial rider
**Tech Comfort:** Low
**Research basis:** [NCC USSD Determination 2025](https://www.ncc.gov.ng/sites/default/files/2025-04/USSD-DETERMINATION-2025.pdf) (banking sessions capped at 120 seconds and charged at N6.98 per transaction, billed to airtime from March 2025), [The Guardian](https://guardian.ng/technology/telecoms/ussd-codes-may-become-inactive-in-14-days-over-banks-n250b-debts/) on CBN USSD volumes (N2.19 trillion across 252.06 million transactions in H1 2024) and an estimate of 88 million feature-phone users.
**In their words:** unverified.

### Goals
- Buy an electricity token and airtime without needing data.
- Send small amounts to family.
- Pay only the set USSD charge per transaction.
- Complete a payment in one short session.

### Frustrations
- USSD sessions time out before he finishes.
- Charges are deducted from airtime, so a failed session still costs money.
- Long menus and English prompts slow him down.
- When a session fails, there is no clear path to recover the money.

### Behaviors
- Dials bank and biller USSD strings as his main payment method.
- Buys airtime frequently and pays for electricity in small amounts.
- Asks a trusted agent to help when a code fails.
- Relies on Hausa and Pidgin, not English.

### Scenario
Musa's electricity token runs out at night. He has no data bundle, so he dials a USSD string. The session times out twice and airtime is deducted for both attempts, which is the part that angers him. With Sabi reachable on the same USSD channel, the session is shorter, the prompt is in Hausa, and a completed payment returns a token and a receipt by SMS. When a session fails, the next session picks up the same pending payment instead of starting over.

## Persona 3: Ifeoma

**Tagline:** A registered small-company owner who needs every payment and receipt to survive tax and audit scrutiny.
**Demographic:** 39, Lagos (Yaba). Runs a printing and packaging business with 12 staff. Degree holder. Android phone and a laptop.
**Role:** SME owner and de facto bookkeeper
**Tech Comfort:** Medium-high
**Research basis:** [PwC Nigeria Tax Reforms 2025](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/nigeria-tax-reform-insight-series-sectoral-analysis.pdf) (small-company exemptions, a 4% development levy above the threshold, higher non-compliance penalties, and phased e-invoicing and VAT fiscalisation), [Businessday](https://businessday.ng/news/article/over-4000-big-companies-yet-to-complete-e-invoicing-system-onboarding-firs/) on the e-invoicing rollout and its later extension to smaller businesses, and [Technext](https://technext24.com/news/ministry-of-interior-%e2%82%a6150k-traps-user/) on a business owner whose agency payment produced a valid receipt but no service.
**In their words:** "I could have sent money to the official directly. I didn't. I chose the official way." (Owoyemi Segun, via Technext)

### Goals
- Pay taxes, levies, and statutory fees before their deadlines.
- Obtain a receipt for every payment that reconciles with the agency's record.
- Avoid penalties for late, missing, or unverifiable payment.
- See which obligations are due and when.

### Frustrations
- A receipt in hand does not mean the agency's system shows the payment.
- Payments have gone to a former account or a vendor the agency no longer uses.
- There is no single view of obligations, deadlines, and status.
- Non-compliance penalties have risen.

### Behaviors
- Uses a bank app for transfers and pays statutory fees close to the deadline.
- Keeps a digital folder of receipts and forwards them to her accountant.
- Is being pulled into e-invoicing and VAT fiscalisation as the rollout expands.
- Delegates errands but keeps payment authorisation herself.

### Scenario
Ifeoma's VAT filing is due and the e-invoicing system now records her supplies. She needs to pay a levy and match it to the return. She asks Sabi what is outstanding, pays from the business account, and gets a receipt stored against the obligation. When an agency's record does not match her receipt, Sabi flags the mismatch and opens a case with the reference instead of leaving her to prove the payment by email.

## Persona 4: Tunde

**Tagline:** A diaspora son who pays his mother's bills and family obligations from abroad and wants proof they were settled.
**Demographic:** 41, London. Works in IT. Sends money home monthly. Keeps a Nigerian bank account and a UK phone.
**Role:** Diaspora remitter and family payer
**Tech Comfort:** High
**Research basis:** [Business Insider Africa](https://africa.businessinsider.com/local/markets/love-loyalty-and-responsibility-why-nigerians-in-diaspora-send-billions-home-each/1sm9bz7) on remittances reaching $20.93 billion in 2024 and family support motives, the [OhentPay 2025 UK-Nigeria report](https://techpoint.africa/brandpress/ohentpay-data-reveals-black-tax-nigerian-diaspora/) (51.4% of large transfers go to mothers; 28% say family support limits their saving), and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/nbcblk/even-1-new-tax-will-burden-african-immigrants-send-money-back-home-rcna224406) on the new 1% US remittance tax.
**In their words:** "Sometime I can send N40,000, sometimes I can send N120,000 to my folks, it's usually within that range." (Gabriel, via Business Insider Africa)

### Goals
- Settle his mother's electricity, medical, and statutory bills directly.
- Get proof that a bill was paid and not diverted.
- Understand the true cost of each transfer.
- Reduce repeated calls with family about money.

### Frustrations
- Money sent for one bill is used for something else.
- Government portals are hard or impossible to pay from abroad.
- Fees and taxes add to the cost, and the new US tax adds more.
- He cannot see whether a payment landed.

### Behaviors
- Uses a remittance app such as Remitly, Nala, or LemFi, and compares rates.
- Calls family to confirm receipt.
- Would pay the biller directly if a channel let him.
- Uses a UK phone and a Nigerian bank account.

### Scenario
Tunde's mother calls about a disconnection notice. He wants to pay the biller, not send cash that may be spent elsewhere. He opens Sabi, pays the electricity bill from his UK number, and sends the receipt to his mother and to himself. The next month, Sabi tells him the bill is due before his mother has to ask.

## Persona comparison

| Device | Mid-range Android | Feature phone | Android and laptop | iPhone and Nigerian bank account |
| Channel | App and WhatsApp | USSD and SMS | App and bank | App |

## Stakeholder personas

### Stakeholder 1: Bola, agency revenue officer
**Role:** Revenue and reconciliation officer at a federal ministry, department, or agency.
**Goal:** Confirm that collections reach the designated treasury account with no deductions and that every receipt is genuine.
**Needs from the system:** reconciliation by reference, no point-of-collection deduction, tamper-evident receipt records, and exception reporting.
**Research basis:** [Arise News](https://www.arise.tv/nigeria-bans-cash-payments-for-all-government-revenues-to-boost-transparency/) and [Businessday](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fg-rolls-out-unified-revenue-platform-in-biggest-overhaul-since-tsa/) on the cashless collection and FTeR rules.
**Feeds:** [nfr](nfr.md) for integrity controls, and [operations](operations.md) for reconciliation and exception reporting.

### Stakeholder 2: Chidinma, compliance and privacy officer
**Role:** Data protection and compliance lead inside Sabi or its licensed partner.
**Goal:** Keep personal data processing lawful and auditable and handle data subject requests on time.
**Needs from the system:** consent capture and withdrawal, access and deletion handling, audit logging, and compliant cross-border transfer rules.
**Research basis:** [Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023](https://ncc.gov.ng/sites/default/files/2024-11/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf) and [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2023/11/Navigating-the-Enforcement-Framework-under-the-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf) on enforcement and penalties up to N10,000,000 or 2% of annual gross revenue.
**Feeds:** [nfr](nfr.md) for privacy controls, and [operations](operations.md) for audit review and data subject handling.

### Stakeholder 3: Emeka, support operations agent
**Role:** Front-line support for failed payments, reversals, and receipt disputes.
**Goal:** Resolve a failed payment or produce a receipt without escalating to the payment partner for every case.
**Needs from the system:** a case queue keyed by reference, transaction status history, reversal and refund workflow, and clear escalation paths.
**Research basis:** [FIJ](https://fij.ng/article/opay-palmpay-keep-publisher-waiting-11-months-after-he-flagged-n480000-taken-by-fraudsters/) and [Business Times](https://businesstimes.ng/2026/07/15/opay-under-fire-over-alleged-146000-pending-transfers/) on unresolved reversals and pending transfers.
**Feeds:** [operations](operations.md) for the case queue and escalation path.

## Anti-personas

**The WhatsApp replacement seeker.** A user who wants a full chat and social network and will judge Sabi against WhatsApp. Sabi will not compete on general messaging, and serving this user would pull effort into a product the evidence says cannot be won here.

**The crypto custody seeker.** A user who wants a stablecoin wallet, escrow, or trading (the positioning that some African messaging apps have taken). Sabi handles payments for real obligations through licensed rails and does not custody or trade crypto, so this user is out of scope by design.
